## The challenge

Outdated software was blocking the sale of a national logistics business. The prospective buyer's chief technology officer said the buyout could not proceed until the system was modernised.

The system ran on servers in a third-party data centre, costing more than £335,000 a month. It also used different technologies and working practices from the buyer, making it harder to bring the two organisations' operations together.

## My role

I led the work as team lead and software architect, reporting to the client's chief technology officer.

I chose technologies and working practices already used by the buyer, so the new system would fit how they operated. I presented the business case to the client's executives, and recruited and led an eight-engineer team to deliver the modernisation. I worked directly with senior leaders from both organisations throughout the project.

I was responsible for project governance, software and cloud architecture, and technical delivery.

## How I worked

The system and its supporting services moved to Microsoft's Azure cloud. I designed the new system to stay available during the move and to handle deployments and disruptions without taking the service offline.

I introduced a shared process for changing project documents, software, and infrastructure. Whether updating training materials, changing a security rule or releasing new software, the proposed change was visible for review before it took effect.

Stakeholders reviewed changes within their own areas of responsibility. For example, security engineers reviewed changes to network access; technical managers reviewed increases in computing capacity and cost; operations managers reviewed software releases. They could assess the consequences for the service without needing to review the underlying code.

Their involvement did not stop at approval. I encouraged those stakeholders to put approved changes into effect themselves, through the same controlled process, rather than hand that step back to engineers.

This gave the people responsible for the service a direct role in changing it. For an operations manager, a software release was no longer something engineers delivered to them. It was a change they understood, approved, and scheduled themselves.

I designed and delivered 120 hours of training for six existing client staff. It covered how the system was built, how to operate it and how to investigate problems, from the basics through to how to troubleshoot faults in the live service. The client team were then able to operate and maintain the system independently.

## Results

- The system routes and tracks over 850,000 parcels per day
- The previous system was costing more than £335,000 a month in hosting costs; the new system reduced costs by 85% to a little over £50,000 a month
- There were no planned or unplanned outages during the transition, nor between the move and the end of my contract in early 2026
- The modernised system addressed the technology blocker to the acquisition, which went ahead successfully
