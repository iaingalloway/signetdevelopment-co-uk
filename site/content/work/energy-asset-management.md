## The challenge

One of the UK's largest energy companies was operating a demand-side management system to coordinate electricity consumers such as EV chargers cooling systems to use more electricity when renewable power was abundant and less when demand was high.

Every release required taking the system offline and manually applying changes. The team had arranged two eight-hour maintenance windows each week, but this meant that overall uptime was below 90% for a service that needed to respond to the national grid in real time.

## My role

I was brought in as technical lead to improve how changes were made and meet an uptime target of 99%.

I led an existing client team of five engineers. I needed to improve reliability without stopping their ongoing work.

I was responsible for project governance and technical delivery.

## How I worked

I automated testing and deployment so releases no longer required taking the system offline or making manual changes.

I also addressed how changes to data reached the system. There were too many steps between making a decision and putting it into effect. This made changes slow and hard to follow, and introduced errors that engineers had to correct. I worked with stakeholders to shorten that process and manage changes to data through the same controlled approach used for software. This made changes quicker, easier to follow, and less prone to error.

## Results

- Deployment-related downtime was eliminated. Overall uptime increased from below 90% to 99.99%, exceeding the 99% target
- Measured engineer time spent correcting data errors fell by 90%, leaving more time to deliver changes
